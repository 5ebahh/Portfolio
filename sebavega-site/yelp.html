Yelp

Make Yelp a place worth coming back to.

Overview

The Problem

Yelp’s monthly app users fell from 33 million to 29 million between 2022 and 2024. Finding a place takes too much scrolling, people doubt whether reviews are real, and there’s no community to keep them coming back.

The Redesign

Five tabs, each with one job: Home, Explore, Create, Map and Profile. Friends and trusted reviewers come first, search and filters get faster, and the map becomes a place to discover, not just a list of pins.

My Role

I was design lead for a five-person team. I set our three checkpoints, split the app into sections across the team and kept the screens consistent from research to the final prototype. I designed the Home tab myself: the feed, highlights, messages and the AI assistant.

Process

From research to a new structure, wireframes, an A/B test and final screens.

  1. 1 Research

    Find Why People Leave.

    We mapped pain points in the current app: ads and sponsored posts, a bland interface, settings and support hidden away, no personalized recommendations, and no way to tell if a reviewer actually visited.

    Then we scored five apps people use to find places, and interviewed people with questions in seven groups: search and discovery, social features, UI and navigation, reviews, customer support, maps, and customization.

  2. 2 Synthesis

    Three Insights, One Objective.

    We wrote down what people told us, then affinity-mapped it to find what kept coming up. Three insights became the base of the redesign.

    1. UI & Navigation

      Navigation

      Key insight

      People want to find and filter relevant places fast, not sift through cluttered layouts and irrelevant results.

    2. Social Features

      Social

      Key insight

      People want recommendations from friends and trusted communities, not anonymous strangers with no context.

    3. Reviews / Feedback

      Reviews

      Key insight

      People want to trust that ratings are genuine and recent, instead of second-guessing reviews that may be fake or outdated.

    4. Ads

      Sponsored listings look like real results and clutter the page.

    5. Details

      Long reviews skip what matters: wait time, price, portions and allergy info.

    6. Business owners

      Owners can’t see how reviews get filtered, or how best to respond.

    Four personas, a high-school athlete who travels for games, a college student who picks the spot for his friends, a retiree new to town and a local business owner, each got a journey map that kept the insights tied to real situations.

    Overall Objective

    How might we streamline the Yelp experience so that discovery, trust and community make it feel like a place worth coming back to?

  3. 3 Direction

    Five Tabs, Each With One Job.

    The current app splits tasks across Search, Projects, Assistant, Collections and Me, and keeps contributions, community and settings in one long list. The new structure gives each task a predictable home, adds a tab for creating, and turns the map into its own destination.

  4. 4 Wireframes

    Draft Every Tab in Mid-Fidelity.

    For our second checkpoint, each of us wireframed our tab in grayscale before adding any color: a home feed of highlights and reviews, the AI assistant, a map you can open anytime, and a profile with history and contributions grouped.

  5. 5 A/B Testing

    Test How People Leave the Map.

    Once the map became its own tab, getting back out of it had to feel natural. We compared five versions: keeping the navbar, an X button, a back button, and two with search at the bottom.

    We carried the version that keeps the navbar into the final design.

Final Design

The finished app: a social home feed, a business page built for deciding and a map to explore.

Before & After

How each part of the app changed, across the whole team’s redesign.

    1. Home My Design

      Before: Ads and sponsored posts fill the feed, with little real value.

      After: Highlights and featured reviews from people you follow, and messages with friends about where to go next.

    2. AI Assistant My Design

      Before: A plain chat that only answers questions about one business.

      After: An assistant for the whole app, with quick prompts like Trending Spots and Date Night, that answers with real places, ratings and short summaries.

    3. Explore

      Designed by Jordan

      Before: Searching means typing and scrolling a long list of suggestions.

      After: Recent and popular searches, quick filters for price, rating, distance and open now, and results that show the deciding details.

    4. Business Page

      Designed by Jordan

      Before: Offers, ordering and tabs crowd the page before the reviews.

      After: Rating, price and distance up top, then featured dishes, a full menu and customer reviews.

    5. Create

      Designed by Ryan

      Before: Writing a review starts with a blank form.

      After: Start with the camera: snap a photo of the place, then turn it into a review or add it to your photos.

    6. Map

      Designed by Sara

      Before: The map only opens after a search, with pins and little else.

      After: A map tab you can open anytime, with layers for hot spots, places your friends visited and your saved spots.

    7. Profile

      Designed by Riddhima

      Before: Contributions, community and settings sit in one long list.

      After: History, contributions and settings grouped, a QR code to share your profile, and collections of saved places.

    Outcome & Reflection

    What Was Delivered

    Research with five competitor scores, interviews, four personas and journey maps; a new five-tab structure; mid-fidelity wireframes; an A/B test of the map; and high-fidelity prototype flows for every tab, presented across three checkpoints.

    What Comes Next

    Test the final flows with Yelp users and measure what the redesign is for: how fast people find a place, and whether highlights from friends make reviews easier to trust.
