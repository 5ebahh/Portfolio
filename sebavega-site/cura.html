Overview

The problem

The team’s secondary research explored trust, representation, geography, finances, and referral practices. Trial information also has to fit the realities of work, caregiving, travel, and the time needed to discuss a decision.

My goal

Help people—especially people of color—consider clinical trials without compromising their commitments and lifestyle. The goal is informed, voluntary choice, including the choice not to participate.

My contribution

The concept, research synthesis, and interface work are presented as a team effort. [add identify my research, design, and testing contributions]

Process

The observations, decisions, and open questions behind the concept.

  1. 1 Research

    Look beyond the person holding the phone.

    We considered race, gender, LGBTQ+ identity, age, economic circumstances, and geography as intersecting lenses. Referring clinicians and trial teams also shape access. These themes came from secondary research, not target-patient interviews.

    [add verified source citations and patient interview evidence]

  2. 2 Exploration

    Compare education, community, and access.

    The team explored physician training, family workshops, and short explainers. The selected Trial Match & Readiness direction brought discovery and education into a mobile guide.

  3. 3 Design

    Let people pause and return.

    Home and Search introduce trials and learning content. Library holds saved information for later consideration. Profile groups participation history. Proposed matching and multilingual capabilities were not demonstrated as working services.

  4. 4 Early feedback

    Treat a small pilot as a starting point.

    A convenience pilot involved three campus friends, not a representative patient sample. Two reported greater willingness after a video; two described navigation as easy. These responses do not establish enrollment, informed consent, or lasting trust.

    [add pilot task wording and explain how feedback changed the design]

Final design

Four connected areas—Home, Search, Library, and Profile—make learning and discovery predictable. The concept leaves room to save information and return when ready.

Outcome & reflection

What was delivered

Delivered an academic mobile concept and a small convenience pilot. The project has not demonstrated reduced disparities, improved recruitment, or clinical outcomes.

What comes next

The next round should involve intended patient communities, clarify geographic and demographic scope, and explore clinician referrals. Practical requirements and understanding matter more than persuading someone to enroll.
